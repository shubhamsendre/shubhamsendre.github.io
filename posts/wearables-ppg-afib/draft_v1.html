What This Is (and Isn’t)

Smartwatches detect atrial fibrillation (AFib) from PPG — the optical pulse waveform measured at the wrist or fingertip — not from ECG. This post compares two ways to do that on the same data: a classical pipeline (detect pulses, measure how irregular the intervals are, fit logistic regression) and a small 1D convolutional network on the raw waveform.

It is not a clinical validation. It is 35 patients, bedside-monitor PPG from ICU patients, and a modest sample. The point is how the two approaches behave when the evaluation is done honestly.

The Data

MIMIC PERform AF (Zenodo 6807403, ODbL) is a derived subset of MIMIC-III: 19 AF and 16 non-AF subjects, 20 minutes each, PPG sampled at 125 Hz. It needs no PhysioNet credentialing. Each recording is cut into non-overlapping 30-second windows, giving 1,398 windows (54% AF) after dropping flat or unusable segments.

Method 1: Pulse-Interval Irregularity (NeuroKit2)

AFib is “irregularly irregular”, so the signal of interest is variability in the beat-to-beat interval. For each window:

  1. nk.ppg_process cleans the PPG and detects pulse peaks.
  2. Inter-beat intervals (IBIs) are kept if they imply 30–200 bpm.
  3. Seven features are computed: RMSSD, SDNN, pNN50, coefficient of variation, Poincaré SD1/SD2, turning-point ratio, mean heart rate.
  4. A class-balanced logistic regression is fit on standardized features.

As a floor, a single-feature detector (RMSSD, min-max scaled) is also scored.

Method 2: 1D CNN on the Raw Waveform

Three conv blocks (16/32/64 channels, kernel 7, BatchNorm, ReLU, max-pool 4), global average pooling, dropout 0.3, one logit. Each window is z-scored. 12 epochs of Adam, no augmentation, no tuning.

Evaluation: Subject-Independent or Not at All

Windows from one person are highly correlated, so a random window split lets a model recognise people instead of rhythm and inflates every number. All results here use 5-fold StratifiedGroupKFold grouped by subject: no patient appears in both train and test. Subject-level results average a subject’s window probabilities and threshold at 0.5.

Results

Everything above was repeated over 20 random seeds (each reshuffles which subjects land in which fold, and re-initialises the CNN). Intervals are 95% subject-level bootstrap intervals (2,000 resamples of the 35 patients, scores averaged over the 20 seeds), so they reflect both having only 35 patients and seed-to-seed variation. Windows are resampled with their patient, never separately.

What the repeats add:

  • The ranking holds. The classical AUROC interval ([0.91, 1.00]) is far from the CNN’s ([0.43, 0.70]).
  • The CNN is consistent with chance. Its AUROC interval includes 0.5. It is also the unstable one: seed-to-seed SD of AUROC is 0.065, versus 0.018 for the classical model.
  • Sensitivity/specificity for the CNN depend on the seed. My first single-seed run happened to give 0.45 / 0.59; averaged over seeds it is 0.75 / 0.38. The CNN is not reliably leaning either way at a 0.5 threshold, which is itself a sign it learned little.
  • The subject-level sensitivity “1.000 [1.000, 1.000]” is not a guarantee. Every AF patient was flagged in the resamples that contained one, but a bootstrap of 19 patients cannot see a failure that did not occur in the data. The specificity interval ([0.72, 1.00]) is the more honest picture.

Then drag the threshold slider to see how the classical detector, the CNN, and RMSSD alone trade sensitivity against specificity.

neurokit2, wfdb, and torch aren’t WASM-safe, so pulse detection and model scores were precomputed offline (105 windows, 2 MB). What is live: the interval features for the window you pick and the threshold sweep.

Why the CNN Loses Here

  • Tiny data. ~28 training subjects per fold. A CNN has to discover “interval irregularity” from raw samples; the classical pipeline is handed it by construction.
  • The signal is about timing between beats, not waveform shape. A global pooled CNN with local kernels has no direct route to inter-beat intervals.
  • Untuned. 12 epochs, no augmentation, no early stopping. A tuned or pretrained model could do better — this is a baseline, not a ceiling.

Note also that RMSSD alone has an AUROC of 0.86 but sensitivity of 0.26 at the default threshold: the ranking is good, the operating point is not. Threshold choice matters as much as the model.

Caveats

  • 35 subjects; even the bootstrap intervals above assume these patients are a fair sample of AF/non-AF ICU patients.
  • ICU bedside PPG is cleaner than wrist PPG during daily motion.
  • Non-AF subjects aren’t necessarily healthy; they may have other arrhythmias or ectopy, which the dataset does not break out.
  • The seed-0 run drives the notebook’s waveform scores and ROC curves; the tables above are the 20-seed summary.

Reproducing

make_waveforms.py exports the notebook’s waveform file; notebook.py is the marimo source. generate_artifacts.py (expects the WFDB zips from Zenodo unpacked into data/raw_af/ and data/raw_nonaf/) writes public/artifacts/windows.csv and results.json. Dependencies: neurokit2, wfdb, scikit-learn, torch, pandas.
